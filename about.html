My teaching philosophy

The Two P's: Patience & Practice

My teaching technique revolves around two simple but powerful principles: Patience and Practice.

I call them my Two P's because I genuinely believe that anyone can learn to play a musical instrument if they follow these two principles consistently.

P1

Patience

Trust the process.

Give yourself time to learn.

Learning music is a journey, not a race. Every student learns at a different pace, and I never expect beginners to master something overnight. There will be mistakes, slow progress and challenging moments. That's completely normal.

I encourage my students to be patient with themselves, enjoy the learning process and understand that every small improvement matters.

P2

Practice

Keep showing up.

Consistency makes the difference.

You don't need to practise for hours every day to improve. What matters is practising regularly and practising correctly.

Even 15–20 minutes of focused daily practice can make a significant difference over time. I encourage students to follow a structured practice routine, revise what they learn in class and gradually build their skills.

Patience + Practice = Progress

My belief

I don't believe in rushing students through lessons just to complete a syllabus. I believe in making sure they understand what they are learning and can confidently apply it.

My responsibility as a teacher is to guide, simplify and motivate. The student's responsibility is to be patient and practise consistently.

You don't need to be naturally talented. You need to be willing to learn.

At The Strings Theory, we don't just teach you to play an instrument. We help you build the patience and discipline to become a musician.

For first-time learners

Why I focus on beginners and first-time music learners

I have always believed that learning music should not feel intimidating. You don't need to have a musical background, extraordinary talent or previous experience to start playing an instrument. You simply need the willingness to learn.

That's why I have a special place for beginners and people who are completely new to music.

Over the years, I have realised that the biggest challenge for a beginner isn't always learning chords, scales or strumming patterns. It's knowing where to start, understanding the basics and staying motivated when things feel difficult.

Many people want to learn guitar or ukulele but give up because they find traditional teaching methods complicated, overwhelming or too theoretical.

I want to change that experience.

My teaching approach focuses on making the first steps in music simple, practical and enjoyable. Instead of rushing through lessons, I believe in building a strong foundation, understanding each concept and progressing at a comfortable pace.

My goal is to help students:

  • Start learning music with zero prior experience.
  • Overcome the fear of holding and playing an instrument.
  • Understand the fundamentals without unnecessary complications.
  • Play their favourite songs as they develop their skills.
  • Build confidence through consistent practice.
  • Develop a genuine love for music rather than simply learning to pass an examination.

I especially enjoy teaching adults, working professionals and people who have always wanted to learn an instrument but never found the right time or guidance.

I believe it's never too late to start learning music. Whether you are six or sixty, a complete beginner or someone returning to music after years, there is always a place to begin.

No experience required. No musical background needed. Just start with your first chord.
